## 5. Cross-experiment consistency

- D (dimension scalability) at n=4 uses the same protocol as A and reproduces the A value
  exactly: λmax = 8.608883 (both groups).
- E (high-dimension literature comparison) uses the common initialization x_i(0)=100i and
  therefore does **not** coincide with D at n=4: proposed λmax = 8.568068 (E) vs
  8.608883 (D/A). This difference is expected and is disclosed in the caption of
  Fig. `fig:high-dimensional-comparison` in the paper.

## 6. Baseline reproducibility

- The 7 four-dimensional baselines of group A and the He / Zhang high-dimension baselines
  of group E reproduce the legacy project's values bit-for-bit (see
  `audit/old_vs_new_results.csv`: 125 unchanged fields).
- **Stale-data finding:** the legacy project's saved CSV rows for `zang_linear_modular`
  (all 9 dimensions) and `yao_complex_pascal` (n=11, 12) disagreed with a fresh
  recomputation. Re-running the *legacy code itself* in the current environment reproduces
  the new values exactly (e.g. Zang n=4: SE = 0.9435152800758925,
  λmax = 0.8668164772678195). Conclusion: those legacy CSV rows were generated by an
  earlier state of the legacy code and are stale; the new results faithfully reflect the
  current, unit-tested implementation. Affected paper locations: the E-group figure
  `fig:high-dimensional-comparison` (Zang and Yao curves) and the discussion ranges;
  qualitative conclusions (grouping, rankings) are unchanged.
